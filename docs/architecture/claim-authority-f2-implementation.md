# F2 — Dimension Authority, Completeness and Policy Evaluator

Status: local, shadow-only implementation for Product review. F3 is not implemented. The frozen Authority & Derivability register remains semantic authority; this document describes executable mechanics, not a new policy decision.

## Boundary and data flow

`evaluateF2({ analysis, graph, attestations, gates, denominators, policy, supersessions, narrowingLinks, accountKey, requestedPositiveClaimIds })` validates the F1 graph against the same canonical snapshot, evaluates claims independently, and returns a frozen `F2Evaluation`. It does not mutate F1 or canonical data. No production consumer imports F2. `tryEvaluateF2` turns a malformed sidecar evaluation into a redacted `unavailable` diagnostic; it does not affect canonical analysis.

The only automatic authority F2 recognizes is a *represented* F1 canonical reference: direct observation with canonical evidence may satisfy the statement-source lane, and a validated calculation reference may satisfy deterministic arithmetic. A derived fee control is not silently treated as a printed-source control. All other F2 attestations are caller-supplied `hand_authored_non_authoritative_test` records. Production/public/private admission, source resolution, storage, corpus selection, and research integration do not exist. The result is explicitly `shadow_only` with `hand_authored_non_authoritative_test` standing; `supported` here is an evaluator verdict for the supplied test proof, **not** a production authority grant.

## Rules and decisions

`rules.ts` has an exhaustive version-pinned rule for each of the 25 F1 dimensions. Rules constrain primary reasoning class, allowed lanes, alternative required lane sets, seven-gate dependencies, required proof facets, and where critical the lane authorized to provide each facet. There is no global source ranking. An unrecognized dimension fails closed. `processor_markup` has a separate strong-claim override; retired `proven_at_cost` and conflated `processor_profit` cannot pass. Fee labels, section position, percentage, discount, QUAL/NQUAL/DISC, or per-item mechanics do not provide the underlying-cost/control facet.

The decision preserves status, a separate `correct_answer | correct_refusal | unresolved | hard_failure` evaluation outcome, admitted *reference/code kind* (never an amount), required and satisfied lanes, missing lane alternatives/facets, blocked gates, attestation IDs, conflicts, policy decision, blocked dimensions, and independent hard failures. F1 candidate-only semantic values are never changed in the graph; only test-only shadow decisions may classify them. Unknown and not-applicable remain explicit. Strong refusal remains `refused`; an explicitly linked and independently supported same-subject `merchant_pricing_component` may produce `partially_supported` for `processor_markup`, with the strong value still unknown and the narrower claim ID retained. A claimed positive when checks do not pass is an `unsupported_positive_admission` hard failure. A correct refusal is a passing semantic outcome, not a hard failure.

The six ownership/control dimensions have separate rule entries and no implicit edges. Statement biller/collector proof cannot satisfy economic beneficiary, contractual controller, price controller, or retained-margin recipient. Private proof requires matching account context and merchant-account scope. Unreviewed private-to-global promotion is a separate hard failure. Public proofs require subject/scope compatibility and effective-period coverage of the analysis interval; a current proof for an older statement fails even when it is the only available proof.

Contradictory F1 claims remain `conflict`, regardless of attestation ordering. Only an explicit fixture-only admitted review record with rule/version, decision ID, and selected winner can resolve the conflict; the losing claim is refused. Product policy uses a separate reviewed-policy lane and a decision record for materiality, visibility, blocking, priority, reportability, or wording/action ceiling. It controls use, not financial value.

## Completeness and denominators

F2 evaluates all seven frozen gates separately: observed-page fact, statement total, fee composition, pricing architecture, comparison, actionability, and savings. Each carries its own required evidence, subject key, and `eligible | partial | blocked | unknown` outcome. A page fact can pass while pricing or savings remains blocked. Claim rules select only the gates relevant to that dimension. Only total, composition, and pricing architecture may be attested at analysis scope; page facts, comparisons, actions, and savings require a matching claim subject. A gate for one subject cannot unlock another.

Accounting, economic-classification, and merchant-facing denominator decisions are also independent. Each requires the frozen separately-accounted-for categories plus a typed canonical reference to an independent *printed gross* fee control. A signed canonical total, derived diagnostic, or missing printed-gross control cannot satisfy these denominator policies. F2 does not calculate coverage amounts or create another owner of money.

## Tested boundaries and remaining limits

The hand-authored tests cover all 25 rule entries, seven gates and three denominator policies against the frozen register; independent owner/control outcomes; broad merchant component versus strong markup/retained profit; narrow reference equality versus contract and retired at-cost meaning; historical back-projection and scope mismatch; policy blocking; conflict/supersession; explicit narrower-claim preservation; recurrence/annualization/savings refusal; all four hard-failure categories; deterministic replay; canonical read-only behavior; test-only provenance rejection; and fail-closed wrapper behavior. Existing F0 tests execute S1–S10 and all 25 global prohibitions. F2 does **not** claim source-backed G1–G8, G6 identity, or G9 acceptance.

The test facets are assertions, not externally verified source facts. F2 does not compute rate equality, independently prove a publisher, resolve source conflicts, select historical reference versions, or inspect merchant contracts. Those require later Product authorization and F3's pinned interfaces. F2 is sufficient to review the evaluator contract and consider F3, but not to authorize customer-facing or production semantic cutover.
